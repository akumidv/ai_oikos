# Scenario: signs of AI generation in a CV

The user asks whether a CV, or a block of experience bullets, was written with an AI model, or
wants their own text to read less generated. The answer is a list of signs with quotes, never a
verdict.

## Steps

1. **Get the text.** Pasted, or read from the file with the `documents` skill.
2. **Go through the groups below.** For each sign found, quote the words and say why it points
   to a model. Skip a group with nothing in it.
3. **Weigh the whole.** One sign means little; five of them across most bullets is a pattern.
   Say how strong the picture is, and that a CV writer produces the same.
4. **Then the use of it.**
   - A candidate's CV: questions for the interview that a generated text cannot answer — the
     numbers before and after, what the person did themselves, what went wrong, why that stack.
   - The user's own CV: concrete edits, each tied to a sign it removes.

## The signs

**Syntax and rhythm**

- One template for every bullet: verb → object → "using X, Y, Z" → a trailing participle
  clause stating the value ("…, establishing…", "…, providing…", "…, while supporting…").
- Paired verbs: "Built and led", "Architected and delivered", "Designed and implemented".
- Lists of four to six items in every bullet, with the Oxford comma.
- Bullets of nearly equal length.

**Words**

- Dense hyphenated compounds: end-to-end, company-wide, role-based, cross-provider, multi-channel.
- Abstract nouns in place of a mechanism: "secure orchestration", "contextual retrieval",
  "delivery management".
- Hedges on claims the model cannot back: "much of", "typically", "up to ~", "readiness".
- Trend terms stacked together (RAG, MCP, multi-agent, model routing) rather than the problem
  they solved.
- Collocations a practitioner would not use ("concurrent heavy users").

**Typography and layout**

- En dash in ranges ("2–10 GB") and a tilde for "about" ("~1M"); people type a hyphen.
- A bullet that lost its marker, or a referent that no longer fits after bullets were
  reordered ("the platform" after a different platform's bullet).

**Content**

- Process instead of results: measurable outcomes in one bullet, none in the rest.
- No context: dates, scale before and after, the person's own part against the team's.
- A shift of voice: impersonal throughout, then a sudden "my".

## Rewriting the user's own CV

- One verb per bullet; drop the participle clause and give the result its own short sentence
  with a number.
- A concrete outcome in each bullet ("research cut from X days to Y hours").
- Plain hyphens; a marker on every bullet; each "the platform" names which.
- Two or three key technologies in a bullet, the rest in Skills.
